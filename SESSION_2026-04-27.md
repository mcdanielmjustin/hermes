## The Pedagogical Pivot (User Framing)

User identified the architectural insight that drove both Phase 6 and
Phase 7:

> "on tier 1 and 2 it makes sense to concede to looser vocab distribution
> OR broader domain vocab pool, but on bloom's 3 and 4 conceding to
> looser vocab distribution may weaken the bloom's level identity."

This re-frames the gate by Bloom's tier identity:
- **T1 (Recognize) / T2 (Understand):** vocabulary IS the test.
  Recognizing "frontal lobe" vs "parietal lobe" is a *valid* recognition
  distinction. Vocabulary divergence is not a tell, it's the mechanism.
- **T3 (Apply) / T4 (Synthesize):** vocabulary divergence IS a tell.
  The LLM uses unique technical terms as a shortcut for the application
  reasoning the question is meant to elicit.

Cluster anchors at T3+ already supply cross-content vocabulary. T1/T2
has no clusters by Bloom's invariant — so the gate (demand-side) and the
extraction pool (supply-side) both needed easing, internal-only at first.

## Phase 6 — Bloom's-Identity-Respecting Threshold Inversion

### Design

Three coordinated changes:

1. **Tier-keyed gate thresholds (Bloom's-inverted):**
   - `KeywordDistributionGate._THRESHOLD_BY_TIER = {1: 5, 2: 4, 3: 3, 4: 3}`
   - `StemKeywordDistributionGate._THRESHOLD_BY_TIER = {1: 3, 2: 3, 3: 2, 4: 2}`
   - `_DEFAULT_THRESHOLD` strict (3 / 2) for missing/unknown tier — so
     malformed questions never bypass the gate.

2. **Broader brief-internal vocabulary pool at T1/T2:**
   `CorrectAnswerFormPlannerAgent.execute` extended at T1/T2 only to
   pull from `primary_core_claims` + `primary_testable_fact` in addition
   to concept descriptions. T3/T4 narrow extraction unchanged
   (cluster anchors are their diversification path).

3. **Orchestrator wiring:** `prepare_task` passes
   `primary_core_claims` and `primary_testable_fact` into the form
   planner's `data` dict.

## Phase 7 — Curated Domain Vocabulary Pool

### Design

User suggestion that drove Phase 7:

> "i was thinking t1 and t2 could have their own feature or column that
> indicates the broader vocab pool for it's domain. i think that would
> be cleaner. it still needs to be curated though."

Architecture (one commit):

1. **Schema:** `data/domain_vocab/{DOMAIN_CODE}.json` per domain with
   `{domain_code, domain_name, curated, version, vocabulary,
   source_summary}`. The `curated: false` flag tracks human-review
   status.

2. **Bootstrap script:** `scripts/generate_domain_vocab.py` walks
   `data/anchor_briefs/{DOMAIN}/*.json` (and `data/concept_vocab/...`
   when present), runs `extract_brief_vocabulary_terms` across every
   brief, frequency-counts, sorts, writes the candidate JSON. Generates
   all 9 domain files in one run; user curates later.

3. **Pipeline wiring:**
   - `pipeline/orchestrator.py:_load_domain_vocab(domain_vocab_dir,
     domain_code)` reads `{dir}/{code}.json` once per (dir, code) tuple,
     caches the term list. Missing file → empty list (degrades cleanly).
   - `prepare_task(...)` accepts new `domain_vocab_dir=None` parameter,
     passes loaded list to form planner.
   - `CorrectAnswerFormPlannerAgent.execute` at T1/T2 only appends
     `domain_vocab` to `permitted_vocabulary` after concept descriptions
     (priority 1) and brief-internal pool (priority 2). Domain pool is
     priority 3.
   - **Cap raise at T1/T2 only:** 8 → 12 (T3/T4 stays 8). Wider pool
     needs landing room.

4. **Prompt enforcement strengthening at T1/T2:**
   `pipeline/prompts.py:build_user_prompt` distractor vocab rule changes
   from "each distractor MUST contain at least ONE of these terms" to
   "each distractor MUST contain at least TWO distinct of these terms"
   — at T1/T2 only. Without this, pool-widening is empirically null
   (LLM picks one term per distractor regardless of pool size).

# Phase 8 onward — extension session (additional 11 commits)

The session continued past Phase 7 + audit. Subsequent work focused on
empirically-driven refinement, then a major architectural shift to per-
tier identity layers. The big insight: T3 (Apply) was the persistent
failure tier, and the existing architecture had been treating it as
"T1/T2 with stricter thresholds." Recognizing T3 has a *different
cognitive identity* (not just a stricter version of recognition) drove
the rest of the work.

## Phase 8 — Canonical-vocab filter at T3+ (`49d22f2`)

D7-PHY-058 Phase 7 calibration came in at 65% pass with 6/7 T2/T3/T4
failures from `keyword_distribution`. Inspecting the flagged words —
`apathy`, `irritability`, `triad`, `fluctuations`, `reflecting` —
revealed the gate was firing on common English descriptors, not
technical synonym tells. Three layered fixes:

1. **Tier-keyed `_MIN_WORD_LEN`**: 5 at T1/T2, 7 at T3/T4 (filters out
   short generic English at apply/evaluate where vocab divergence is
   the tell)
2. **Generic-English exclusion set** in `pipeline/stopwords.py`:
   `GENERIC_DESCRIPTORS` includes "rapid", "triad", "weakness",
   "reflecting", "impaired", etc. — applied at T3/T4 only
3. **Canonical-vocabulary filter**: at T3/T4, restrict the keyword
   gate to count only words in (curated domain pool ∪ brief concept
   descriptions). Generic English doesn't count

Result: D7-PHY-058 climbed to 90% (was 65%); D7-PHY-209 to 95%; T3+T4
combined hit 100% (was 60%). Cost: $0.28/usable Q.

## Phase 9 — TopicRealmGate replaces KeywordDistributionGate (`754d0df`)

User insight: vocabulary uniqueness in correct isn't a tell as long as
distractors engage the topic realm. The actual concern is when
distractors stray off-topic, leaving correct as the only on-realm
option. KeywordDistributionGate's "unique-word counting" approach was
producing false positives on legitimate questions where all 4 options
engaged the realm via different vocabulary.

Built `TopicRealmGate`:
- Realm = (concept-description content words ∪ stem content words)
- Per-option Jaccard with realm
- Fire when correct's overlap exceeds mean of distractors' by tier-
  keyed gap threshold (T1: 0.50, T2: 0.40, T3: 0.30, T4: 0.25)
- Distractor-mean floor (≥0.5 bypass) eliminates false positives where
  correct is exceptionally dense but distractors ARE in the realm

Deregistered `KeywordDistributionGate` from the production pipeline.
Class kept in code for reference; no tests removed.

Followup fixes (`ffd7ba7`, `86f5d9d`, `3e4cdd3`):
- Auto-strip batch helper for "X because Y" reasoning markers when
  multiple options share the pattern (single-pass strip refused due to
  relative-length guard)
- Bloom's gate verb drift: `BloomsCognitiveLevelGate._T3_APPLY_INDICATORS`
  didn't recognize `infer`/`select` even though `_VERB_POOL[3]` permits
  them
- TopicRealmGate distractor-mean floor (above)
- Diagnostic logging captures stem + option text on validation failures

Combined empirical: 91.7% across D7-PHY-058 + D7-PHY-209, $0.29/Q.

## T3 Apply-Identity Layer (`3e82276`)

After Phase 8/9, T3 was still the failure tier (67% across 3 anchors).
The architectural shift: T3 isn't "T1/T2 with stricter thresholds";
T3 has its own cognitive identity demanding **scenario + concept +
forward action** simultaneously. The other tiers' identities have
fewer simultaneous structural requirements.

Three coordinated changes (prevention + validation):

1. **Trim `STEM_PATTERNS[3]`**: removed `error_identification`. The
   "find-error" pattern produced "alternative diagnoses" distractors

## T3 mechanism-presence check (`964a34d`)

Empirical drift discovery in saved D7-PHY-058 batch: 3 of 5 saved T3

```
"Predict ongoing rapid mood fluctuations as the lability component of the frontal triad"
"Predict rapid mood fluctuations alongside the rest of the frontal injury triad"
```

The verb `Predict` is forward-action, but the answer just NAMES the

Empirical: D7-PHY-058 = 18/20 with 3 saved T3 questions all carrying

## Key architectural insights from this session

1. **Tier-as-identity, not tier-as-strictness.** The original
   architecture treated Bloom's tiers as the same kind of question
   with different difficulty thresholds. The shift to "each tier has
   its own cognitive identity demanding different structural
   enforcement" was the unlock for T3 specifically and produced the
   curated identity layer architecture.

2. **Prevention is cheaper than validation.** Adding requirements to
   the initial prompt (`mechanism_marker_clause`, "TWO distinct vocab
   terms" in Phase 7) drops retry frequency more effectively than gate
   pressure alone. Both layers are needed; prevention does most of
   the work; validation is the safety net.

3. **Each tier needs different-shaped enforcement.** T1's check is the
   *inverse* of T3's. T2's check is about complexity *ceiling*. T4's
   check is about complexity *floor*. T3's check requires *all three*
   structural elements simultaneously. Calling this "architectural
   symmetry" was wrong framing — it's *curated structure*, where each
   tier's enforcement is shaped to its specific cognitive demands.

4. **Empirical-first refinement.** Every gate threshold was tuned
   against actual generated content (D7-PHY-058 / D7-PHY-209 / D7-PHY-
   06-2). Theoretical perfection ≠ production fitness. The
   distractor-mean floor (0.5), gap thresholds (0.25-0.50), Jaccard
   threshold (0.10), mechanism-marker patterns — all reflect
   empirical calibration, not first-principles.

## Phase 9 — Stem-fact integrity defense (commit `8462704`)

### Trigger

Manual review of saved batches surfaced an architectural pattern
beyond Bloom's-identity drift: distractors that *contradict facts
already stated in the stem*. User-flagged example (Lester Nichols /
D7-PHY-076):

> Stem: "After bilateral hippocampal damage, Lester Nichols cannot
> form new declarative memories but still recalls his wedding from
> a decade earlier."
> Distractor C: "Retrograde amnesia erases all pre-injury memories
> regardless of when they were consolidated."

User framing: *"the question literally tells the student that lester
remembers his wedding. so why would the student pick c?"*

The student rejects C by reading the stem alone — no content knowledge
required. The misconception target was meant to be a content
distinction, but the LLM picked the cheapest wrong claim by inverting
a stated fact. The pedagogy fails: the question tests reading
comprehension, not the cognitive level it's labeled with.

### Sonnet audit (D7-PHY-076)

Built `scripts/audit_stem_contradictions.py` (Sonnet 4.6, async,
$0.005/Q) for batch-level detection of stem-eliminable distractors.
Initial audit on 18 saved D7-PHY-076 questions:

| Tier | Flagged Q / Total |
|---|---|
| T1 | 0/4 (0%) |
| T2 | 2/5 (40%) |
| T3 | 3/4 (75%) |
| T4 | **5/5 (100%)** |

Flag rate scaled hard with tier — richer stems pack more facts to
contradict. T4 hit ceiling: every audit-eligible synthesis question
had at least one stem-eliminable distractor.

### Three-layer architecture

Implemented as one commit (`8462704`):

**Layer A — prompt prevention** (`pipeline/prompts.py`).
Distractor section gains a clause forbidding distractors from
inverting stated stem facts (laterality, named subject's preserved
abilities, observed findings, onset details), redirecting the LLM
to mechanism-based wrongness. Concrete examples cite the audit
patterns ("bilateral" → "unilateral injury"; "still rides a bicycle"
→ "loss of cycling"; "recalls his wedding" → "all pre-injury
memories erased"). Tier-agnostic — applies wherever focused-mode
distractor planning is active.

**Layer B — heuristic gates** (`pipeline/gates.py`).
Two narrow regex-based gates:

- `LateralityIntegrityGate`: stem 'bilateral' (exclusive) →
  distractor 'unilateral' = fail. Symmetric for the inverse.
  Bypasses when stem references both/neither laterality.
- `UniversalDenialGate`: distractor pairs universal quantifier
  ('all/every/regardless/entirely') with denial verb ('erase/lose/
  abolish/impair') within ~80 chars in either order, with at least
  one shared content word ≥6 chars to the stem. Avoids false
  positives when the universal-denied category doesn't overlap
  what the stem preserves.

Pipeline registration: `LateralityIntegrityGate()` and
`UniversalDenialGate()` added to `create_gate_pipeline()` between
`StemKeywordDistributionGate()` and `DistractorMixGate()`.

**Layer D — Sonnet audit (Layer C skipped intentionally)**.
The pre-ship batch validation runs `scripts/audit_stem_contradictions.py`
(Sonnet 4.6, max_tokens=2048). Catches the broader semantic class:
"selective amnesia" implying preservation, "destroyed both" → "surviving
contralateral" semantic inversions, named-instance preservation that
the regex can't anchor on without literal lexical overlap.

Layer C — an inline LLM validation gate — was deferred. Per-Q LLM
calls would 4× retry cost; batch-level Sonnet audit at $0.005/Q is
sufficient.

## Phase 10 — Criterion-application markers (commit `b695ecb`)

### Trigger

CPAT D3-PPA-034 was the lone regression in the Phase 9 cross-anchor
calibration: 90% → 75% pass rate, with 5/5 T3 questions lost to
`apply_identity` (mechanism marker missing). The content domain is
ADHD/autism diagnostic-criteria checking — fundamentally
threshold-evaluation ("did the patient meet the rule?"), not
mechanism-application ("predict X via biological mechanism Y").
The Phase 8 T3 mechanism mandate doesn't fit criteria-driven
content. The criterion IS the causal anchor for the determination.

### Architecture

`ApplyIdentityGate._MECHANISM_MARKERS` regex extended to accept
criterion-application markers alongside mechanism markers:

- `based on [X]`
- `given that [X]`
- `(satisfies|satisfying|meets|meeting|fails|failing|exceeds|exceeding) [up-to-30-chars] (criterion|criteria|threshold|cutoff|requirement|standard)`
- `for (meeting|failing|satisfying|exceeding) [criterion-language]`

The 30-char tolerance allows phrasings like "satisfying the age-12
onset threshold" where intervening words sit between the verb and
the criterion noun.

`pipeline/prompts.py:mechanism_marker_clause` (T3-only) updated with
both marker classes plus an explicit substitution directive: when
the LLM wants to write `because Y`, instead write `based on Y` /
`satisfying the [criterion]` (avoids OptionClaimGate auto-strip
silently leaving bare labels).

`_GATE_GUIDANCE["apply_identity"]` updated with the same expanded
guidance for retry prompts.

### Empirical validation

Single-anchor regen on D3-PPA-034:

| Run | Pass | T1 | T2 | T3 | T4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 9 (pre) | 15/20 (75%) | 5/5 | 5/5 | **0/5** | 5/5 |
| Phase 10 v1 (gate only) | 15/20 (75%) | 5/5 | 5/5 | **0/5** | 5/5 |
| Phase 10 v2 (+ prompt) | 14/20 (70%) | 5/5 | 2/5 | **2/5** ✓ | 5/5 |

T3 recovered 0/5 → 2/5 — Phase 10 achieves its narrow goal. The 2
saved T3 questions use the exact criterion-application markers the

The headline -5pts is T2 sample variance (stem-length and
topic-realm failures unrelated to Phase 10), not regression. The 2
T3 survivors are pedagogically more valuable than the 1 lost T2

## Phase 11 — Audit-fix loop with Sonnet rewrites (commit `256eb61`)

### Trigger

Sonnet audit on saved batches (the Layer D from Phase 9) produced a
list of stem-eliminable distractors per Q. The audit was passive —
it reported, didn't act. User asked to close the loop: feed the
citations back to Sonnet as rewrite directives.

### Architecture

`scripts/audit_stem_contradictions.py` gains an opt-in `--fix` flag.
After auditing, for each flagged distractor:

1. Build a `FIX_PROMPT` with the original stem, correct option
   (preserved), other distractors (preserved), the flagged
   distractor's text, its misconception_id, and the audit's
   citation (`contradicted_stem_fact`, `explanation`).
2. Call Sonnet 4.6 to rewrite the distractor's `text` and
   `explanation`. The misconception_id, slot, letter, is_correct,
   and concept_id are preserved verbatim.
3. Patch in place, save the modified batch as
   `{input}.fixed.json` alongside the original.

Cost: ~$0.005 per flagged distractor (matches the audit's
per-question cost).

### Empirical ceiling (documented honestly)

Single-pass on BPSY D7-PHY-021 (16-Q batch with 15 flagged
distractors):

| Pass | Audit flags | Distractor count |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-fix | 15 (run 1) / 17 (run 2) | 15 / 17 |
| Post-fix v1 (simple prompt) | 14 | 14 |
| Post-fix v2 (strengthened with all-flags context) | **17** ✗ | 17 |
| Post-fix v3 (reverted to simple) | 16 | 16 |

The fix produces genuinely-different rewrites (verified by diffing
distractor text) but the audit-flag count stays within ±1 of the
pre-fix value. Sonnet swaps one stem-contradiction for another in
fact-dense T3/T4 stems where the misconception target's surface area
intersects multiple stated stem facts.

Iterating doesn't converge — pass-2 of `--fix` on `.fixed.json`
churned 14→15 (more re-flagged than reduced).

The strengthened prompt experiment (giving Sonnet ALL flag context
upfront) backfired: the added context lured Sonnet into longer,
more elaborate rewrites that intersected MORE stem facts (15 → 17).
Reverted.

### Architectural truth

Rewrite-based fixing has marginal empirical value when the residual
is semantic and the same LLM that produced the original is asked
to fix it. The clean catch is the audit (Sonnet flags); the rewrite
needs a different agent (human reviewer or different model class)
to converge.

### Honest framing in the docstring

`--fix` is documented as cleanup-before-human-review, NOT a
one-shot eliminator. The semantic residual that Sonnet flags but
doesn't reliably rewrite away is the architectural ceiling for
prompt-based fixing.
